The Story

Every mission to the Moon and Mars left something behind. Here is why, and why it still matters.

Why we explore

Exploration answers questions no laboratory on Earth can: how worlds form, whether Mars once held water, and how to keep people safe far from home.

Why hardware stays

Fuel is the limit. Every kilogram carried back to Earth costs more propellant, so Apollo crews left equipment on the surface to make room for lunar samples. Robotic missions were never designed to return.

What happens to it

With no liquid water and almost no atmosphere on the Moon, hardware weathers slowly from temperature swings, ultraviolet light and micrometeoroids. On Mars, dust covers solar panels and ends many missions.

Why preserve it

These sites are the first human and robotic footprints on other worlds. In 2011 NASA published recommendations for protecting the historic and scientific value of U.S. lunar artifacts.

    Science and Discoveries

    Lunar geology

    Apollo crews returned 382 kg of samples. Their study supports the view that the Moon formed after a giant impact early in Solar System history.

    Mars geology

    Opportunity found hematite concretions, Curiosity found an ancient lake environment in Gale Crater, and Perseverance explores a river delta in Jezero Crater.

    Space radiation

    Curiosity carries a radiation detector that measured exposure on the trip to Mars and on the surface, data that informs future crew safety.

    Planetary exploration

    InSight’s seismometer recorded over 1,300 marsquakes, revealing Mars’s crust, mantle and core. Viking’s life-detection experiments set the questions later missions still pursue.

    Engineering

    Opportunity was built for about 90 sols and worked for nearly 14 years. Ingenuity, planned for five flights, completed 72.

    Historical preservation

    Descent stages, rovers and instruments are the only physical record of the first crewed and robotic landings. Leaving them in place keeps that record intact.

    EVERY EXPLORER LEAVES A LEGACY.

    A descent stage in the Sea of Tranquility, a rover silenced by dust, a cache of sample tubes waiting in Jezero: none is waste. Each is a record of what people and machines dared to do, and a guide for what comes next. Behind this text: the Moon, Mars and deep space, as an artistic view.
